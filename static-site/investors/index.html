01 / THE PROGRESSION

Build capability before taking on more capital.

The strategy expands from trades we know into responsibility for an entire property. Each step needs its own delivery evidence.

  1. 01Current activity

    Specialist works

    Electrical installations, roofing and facades. This is the operational base for the next stage.

    Evidence: work portfolio and agreed scopes.
  2. 02Next stage

    Complete renovation

    Coordinate the building envelope, services and finishes under one project plan, with defined responsibilities for every trade.

    Gate: a costed pilot, delivery team and acceptance criteria.
  3. 03Longer-term ambition

    Acquire. Develop. Sell.

    Buy a property or site, renovate or build, complete the technical and legal handover, then sell the finished asset.

    Gate: proven delivery, acquisition due diligence, funding and a supported exit.
02 / THE OPPORTUNITY

Existing buildings. Work that changes their usefulness.

Our investment thesis is to create value through the physical improvement of a property: its envelope, installations, layout and quality of use. A purchase has to make sense before relying on market growth.

The envelope

Roof and facade condition affect water ingress, insulation and the durability of the work that follows.

The installations

Electrical systems and coordinated building services are part of a property’s practical usability and handover.

The whole building

Complete renovation brings these scopes together. It also adds coordination, permitting and budget risk that must be priced.

A regional market, with regional rules.

Flanders requires many new owners of homes with EPC label E or F to achieve at least label D within six years of transfer. Exceptions apply. Brussels and Wallonia have separate rules; every property needs its own assessment.

03 / INVESTMENT APPROACH

Start with one property. Make the decision inspectable.

We are open to discussions with capital partners and property owners about a defined renovation pilot. The proposed approach is to agree the asset, work and decision rights before committing funds.

01

Arcadian

Define and price the execution scope, organise delivery and document progress. Appoint qualified designers and specialist partners where required.

02

Capital or property partner

Bring project capital or a suitable property, review the investment case and agree the financing, governance and exit terms.

03

Independent specialists

Check valuation, title, permits, taxes, insurance and technical assumptions. Their responsibilities are defined for the specific project.

Terms to settle before funding

  • A defined asset, project budget, contingency and funding schedule.
  • A legal structure, potentially a dedicated project company, with agreed responsibilities and reporting.
  • Milestone-based funding, change approvals, investor decision rights and any security.
  • Separate disclosure of construction fees, financing costs and the allocation of any profit or loss.

These are proposed working principles. No investment amount, return, security package or project vehicle is being offered on this page.

04 / PROJECT ECONOMICS

Work back to a defensible purchase price.

For a selected property, work back from a conservative sale appraisal and include the complete cost of getting there.

Conservative sale proceeds

− acquisition taxes, notary and transaction costs

− renovation or construction, design and permits

− finance, holding, insurance and selling costs

− contingency and required project margin

= maximum supportable purchase price

An appraisal framework, not a valuation or a return forecast. Tax treatment and all costs require project-specific verification.

What a project dossier should contain

  • Property, title, condition, energy assessment and permit position.
  • Comparable sales, independent appraisal and identified buyer market.
  • Trade-by-trade scope, quotations, contingency and programme.
  • Monthly cash flow, funding sources, fees, taxes and decision rights.
  • Base and downside cases, exit route and the consequences of a delayed sale.

Test the downside together

−10%

Illustrative stress assumptions to test separately and in combination. They are neither forecasts nor acceptance thresholds.

Risks the model must carry

Hidden defects

Survey structure, moisture, asbestos and ground conditions; budget for unresolved findings.

Permits & delivery

Check planning, co-ownership and approvals before relying on the programme. Define responsibilities and change control.

Sale & financing

Allow for cost overruns, interest and slower sales. A rental or refinancing alternative requires its own viable case.

06 / EUROPEAN REFERENCES

Established approaches. Lessons for a smaller first step.

Independent examples of renovation and redevelopment. These are not Arcadian projects, partners or financial comparables.

Ekla / Revive

Revive redeveloped a former brewery site opposite Brussels West Station into a mixed-use residential project.

Lesson for our strategy: assess transport, local demand and the final use alongside the building works.

ANTWERP, BELGIUM

’t Groen Kwartier / Matexi

The former military hospital site was transformed into a neighbourhood combining homes, other uses and a car-free park. Matexi lists the project period as 2003–2017.

Lesson: adaptive reuse requires a coherent plan and patient phasing; the full cycle can be long.

BORDEAUX, FRANCE

Grand Parc / Lacaton & Vassal, Druot, Hutin

The transformation of 530 occupied homes retained the existing structures and added winter gardens and balconies.

Lesson: assess what can be retained before choosing demolition. This social-housing project is not a resale-return benchmark.
